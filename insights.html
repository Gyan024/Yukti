Structuring for Scale
First-Loss Capital: The Most Misunderstood Tool

First-loss capital absorbs initial losses in a portfolio, protecting senior commercial investors. When correctly sized — typically 10–20% of the portfolio — it can mobilise 4–8x in commercial capital per rupee of concessional funding.

Risk-Sharing Mechanisms
Guarantee Mechanisms in India: What Works

India has launched over 50 credit guarantee schemes in the past decade. Most have underperformed. The difference between success and failure lies almost entirely in design, not intent.

Design Failure vs. Design Success

What Fails
  • Blanket coverage without risk differentiation
  • No skin-in-the-game for lenders
  • Opaque claim processes
  • No learning loop or iteration
What Works
  • Partial coverage (50–70%) with lender co-investment
  • Portfolio-level triggers, not loan-level
  • Transparent, fast claim settlement
  • Built-in monitoring and adaptation

MSME Finance
The $530 Billion MSME Credit Gap

India's 63 million MSMEs contribute 30% of GDP but access less than 16% of formal credit. The gap — estimated at $530 billion — cannot be closed by commercial lending alone.

Why MSMEs Cannot Access Credit

No formal financial history
Insufficient collateral
High due diligence cost relative to loan size
Perceived sector risk — lenders avoid

The Blended Solution

Without Blending
  • Commercial rates unaffordable
  • Lenders avoid small tickets
  • MSMEs rely on informal credit at 36–60% rates
With Blending
  • First-loss reduces lender risk
  • Guarantee enables smaller tickets
  • Affordable credit at 14–18% rates

DFI Strategy
DFIs: From Anchor Investor to Market Builder

Development Finance Institutions have historically deployed capital on a deal-by-deal basis. This approach is necessary but insufficient for building deep, liquid impact capital markets in India.

The Market-Builder Playbook

1. Fund proof-of-concept structures in new sectors
2. Standardise documentation and risk frameworks
3. Publish learning openly — create shared infrastructure
4. Step back as commercial capital steps in
